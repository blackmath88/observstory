# Concept: the brutalist observatory creature

Status: north-star concept, round 3 of image exploration (31 Oct 2026). It is not final art, and
not yet pixel art.

The core idea is a gentle, armoured reptile with a modern observatory on its back. It sits under
a twilight sky and is built from heavy concrete and patinated metal. Soft nature, a kind living
creature and monumental architecture meet in one body.

The product meaning is unchanged. Observstory observes, and the creature is its face:
- **resting:** nothing needs a conversation;
- **up:** an overlap or wait exists, and the lantern is lit;
- **travelling:** replay is playing.

## Structural breakdown (from the owner's notes)

1. **Base creature and style.** A massive, friendly turtle/salamander lying low in an alpine
   meadow. Soft-shaded 2D storybook concept art, clean linework, subtle moss, warm lamp light.
   Palette: muted sage, weathered slate-grey concrete, patinated copper, a soft violet twilight
   and snow-capped mountains.
2. **Observatory.** A modern astronomical dome replaces the shell dome. Its curved shutters
   (inspired by R1) open to reveal a steel truss and telescope.
3. **Brutalism.** Vertical, crown-like concrete spires flank the dome, inspired by Soviet
   monuments.
4. **Head and eyes.** The head stays organic, with warm round porthole windows for eyes, not
   concrete visors.
5. **Body.** Riveted plates, moss patches, an iron ladder, a balcony rail, and an armillary
   sphere and weathervane as small brass jewellery.

## Why it's "almost, but not quite"

Our read of 03 and 04:

- **The spires steal the silhouette.** The crown of spikes makes it read as a castle or a crown
  before it reads as an observatory. It also hides the most beautiful part, the opening dome and
  the truss inside it.
- **The brutalism is bolted on, not built in.** The concrete appears as extra towers rather than
  as the creature's own material and massing.
- **The saucer head (03) loses the being.** Slit eyes under a visor read as a bunker. The
  porthole eyes (04) must stay.
- **The mechanism is under-drawn.** The modern, precise truss telescope is the one high-tech
  element. It should be visible and pale, like a skeleton inside the shell.

## The synthesis: brutalism as anatomy

Fold each brutalist reference into a body part instead of adding it on top:

| Reference | Becomes | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| R3, curved concrete shell-fins | **The dome's two shutters.** Two or three overlapping curved concrete shells that part like a beetle's wing cases. | One gesture is both architecture and anatomy. Opening the shells is waking up. |
| R1, truss telescope | **The skeleton inside.** A pale steel lattice telescope with a round mirror, visible only when the shells part. | A modern, precise heart inside a soft creature. |
| R2, saucer ring with ribbon windows | **The collar** where the dome meets the shell: a cantilevered concrete ring with a band of recessed windows glowing amber. | The ribbon windows become a breathing light band, and their glow can carry state. |
| Spires | Reduced to **two or three low fins** at the rear, like dorsal plates, or removed. | Keeps the brutalist rhythm without crowning it. |
| Copper | **Seams, rivets and patches only.** The plates themselves are concrete and slate. | Less brown, more stone and sage. |

**Silhouette test:** in black only, it must read as *a tortoise with an observatory dome*,
not a castle.

## Translating brutalism into the product UI

Brutalism here means **honest mass and structure**, not hostility. The UI stays clean, readable
and overview-first (round 3). Brutalism shapes the frame, and the creature keeps it warm.

1. **Massing over decoration.** A few large, monolithic panels instead of many small cards.
   Separation comes from weight and recess, not shadows.
2. **Recessed, not raised.** Content sits *in* the slab: panels are inset, with a darker inner
   edge, like windows cast into concrete. The selected item is the lit window.
3. **The cantilevered header.** The top bar overhangs the content by a few pixels and casts a
   flat shadow line, like the R2 saucer. Tabs are cut into its edge.
4. **Ribbon windows.** One horizontal band of recessed slots runs across the overview, one slot
   per lane:
   - lit (amber) means work is in flight;
   - dark means quiet;
   - a counted, labelled slot means something needs attention.
   This is the R2 window band as a meaningful readout. Every slot is also labelled in text.
5. **Expansion joints.** Section dividers are 2px joints with a 1px highlight, like cast seams.
   Corners are square, with one signature **45° chamfer** on primary panels.
6. **Monumental labels, quiet body.** Section titles are condensed uppercase with wide tracking,
   like inscribed letters. Body text and data stay a neutral sans and mono at GitHub density.
   One display face only, used sparingly.
7. **Material palette:**
   - concrete in shadow for the grounds (night): `#1b1d22`, `#24272e`, `#2f333b`;
   - board-formed grey for the lines: `#3c414b`;
   - a warm lamp amber for "lit": `#f0b35a`;
   - sage and verdigris for selection and focus: `#6fae9a`;
   - the signal orange stays reserved for "needs a conversation": `#ff9550`.
   - No pure white. Ink is warm concrete-paper: `#e8e4dc`.
8. **Texture only at the edges.** Board-form grain and aggregate speckle appear only in margins and
   empty space, as pixel ornament (the same law as before), never behind reading surfaces.
9. **Wholesome counterweight.** Moss and ivy pixel accents grow on quiet panel edges. More moss on
   quieter, older areas is a decorative reward, never data.

row, or sit above the board as a summary strip?
